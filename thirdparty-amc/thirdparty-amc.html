1. Research Programme — Passive Seakeeping

The proposed research focus: Passive heading stability and seakeeping of large low-speed surface vessels — hull form, appendage, and superstructure effects.

This is genuinely novel research territory. Most seakeeping literature assumes powered vessels with active heading control. A large vessel relying entirely on passive directional stability in storm conditions — no engine, no rudder, no crew intervention — has almost no dedicated literature. The research questions are:

Research questionMethod
What hull form asymmetries produce passive weathervaning in following seas?Model testing — paired hull variants; CFD
What appendage configurations maximise roll and pitch damping without active control?Free decay tests; towing tank
How does superstructure placement affect passive heading stability?Wind tunnel; model testing with superstructure variants
Bow-first vs stern-first into weather — which produces better seakeeping at Ark scale?Model testing; seakeeping analysis
What is the interaction between weathervaning (wind-driven) and wave-following behaviour when they conflict?Combined wind/wave model tests
Can moon pool geometry be tuned as a passive pitch/roll damper?CFD; model testing with moon pool variants

These questions are independently valuable to maritime engineering regardless of the specific vessel motivating them. Practical applications include:

  • Wind-assist cargo shipping — large vessels under sail or kite power need passive heading behaviour in storm conditions when propulsion is reduced
  • Disabled vessel drift prediction — search and rescue, towage assessment
  • Survival craft design — passive heading stability of large life rafts and survival vessels
  • Offshore platform weathervaning — turret-moored FPSOs and passive heading systems

The Research Proposal — Without Mentioning the Ark

The AMC research proposal would not mention Noah's Ark. The passive seakeeping topic is independently valuable and fundable on its own terms:

Proposed title: "Passive Heading Stability of Large Low-Speed Unpowered Surface Vessels: Hull Form, Appendage, and Superstructure Effects in Organised Following Sea Conditions"

This is genuinely novel research territory. Most seakeeping literature assumes powered vessels with active heading control. An unpowered vessel relying entirely on hull form asymmetry for directional stability has almost no dedicated literature. The research has direct applications to:

  • Wind-assist cargo shipping — passive heading in storm conditions with reduced propulsion
  • Disabled vessel drift prediction — search and rescue, towage assessment
  • Survival craft design — passive heading of large life rafts
  • FPSO weathervaning — passive heading systems in offshore engineering

Once institutional relationships are established, the specific historical case study can be introduced naturally. Engineers are empiricists, not ideologues. A passive weathervaning result is interesting regardless of which vessel first motivated the question.

Test programme:

Test typeWhat it measures
Free decay — rollNatural roll period; damping ratio ζ; effect of skegs
Free decay — pitchNatural pitch period; damping ratio
Seakeeping — regular wavesRAOs for heave, pitch, roll at various headings
Seakeeping — irregular wavesResponse in JONSWAP spectrum; comparison with Hong et al.
Weathervaning — combined wind/wavePassive heading in organised sea; comparison box vs Magnuson hull
Acceleration measurementVertical and lateral accelerations amidships — animal welfare criterion

Animal welfare criterion: Vertical acceleration limit ~0.6g (airborne threshold for livestock); lateral acceleration limit ~0.3–0.4g (loss of footing). These are biologically justified limits, not passenger comfort standards. Hong et al. used 0.34g vertical at the bow — a passenger ship criterion applied at the worst location. Amidships animal welfare limits are more appropriate and less conservative.

5. Mass Timber Construction Connection

The Ark Encounter (Answers in Genesis, Williamstown KY, opened 2016) is one of the largest timber-framed structures built in the modern era — approximately 155m long, four decks, large-section timber throughout. The construction team, led by builder Leroy Troyer, essentially rediscovered large-scale timber joinery techniques that had not been used at this scale for a century.

Mass timber construction is experiencing a significant revival:

6. Wind-Assist Shipping Connection

Wind-assist cargo shipping is an active field — Cargill, Michelin, and various technology companies are developing rotor sails, rigid wing sails, and kite systems for large cargo vessels. Key engineering questions include:

  • Passive heading behaviour in storm conditions with reduced propulsion
  • Interaction between wind propulsion forces and wave-induced motions
  • Seakeeping at slow speeds with large windage areas

These are essentially the same questions as Noah's Ark passive seakeeping — same scale, same passive heading problem, same storm survival requirement. The research is directly applicable to modern sustainable shipping without any theological framing.
