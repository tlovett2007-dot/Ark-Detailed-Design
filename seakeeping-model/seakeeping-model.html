Physical model testing provides empirical validation of the analytical seakeeping work — particularly roll damping, weathervaning response, and comparative performance between ship-shaped and box hull forms. Two test venues are planned: a local Blue Mountains lake for free decay testing, and the AMC Model Test Basin in Launceston for wave and wind testing.

Modular Design

The end sections are interchangeable — different bow and stern forms can be fitted to the same parallel midsection. This allows direct comparative testing of hull form variants (ship-shaped vs box) under identical conditions with the same instrumented hull. The bilge radius is also designed for swap-out — rectangular, chamfered, and fully radiused cross-sections can all be tested.

The Bathtub Problem

The first water test was conducted in a bathtub — the obvious starting point for a 1800mm model. It failed for a classic reason: the bathtub generated its own resonant oscillation (seiche) that swamped the hull roll signal.

A seiche is a standing wave in a confined body of water, oscillating at the natural resonant frequency of the container. A standard bathtub (~1.5m long) has a seiche period of roughly 0.8 seconds. The model's natural roll period at 1:79 scale is approximately 22/√79 ≈ 2.5 seconds. The two periods are close enough to interact — the recorded data showed two overlapping oscillations, one the hull and one the bathtub. The result was uninterpretable.

Body of waterApproximate seiche periodVerdict
Bathtub (~1.5m)~0.8sToo close to model roll period — unusable
Swimming pool (25m)~10sWell separated — usable
LakeMinutes to hoursNegligible — ideal for free decay
AMC basin (35m)~14sWell separated — usable

The lesson is straightforward: free decay tests require a body of water whose seiche period is well separated from the model's natural roll period. A lake or large pool eliminates the problem entirely.

Sensor Upgrade — Arduino IMU

The 2004 gyroscopic mouse sensor is replaced by a modern Arduino-based IMU (inertial measurement unit). This is a direct sensor swap — the mounting bracket concept is unchanged, the capability is substantially improved.

Parameter2004 gyroscopic mouseArduino MPU-6050 IMU
Angular measurementYes — roll, pitch, yawYes — 3-axis gyro + 3-axis accelerometer
Linear accelerationNoYes — heave, sway, surge
Sample rate~30Hz (USB mouse polling)Up to 1000Hz

Data loggingPC via USB — tetheredSD card — untethered
PowerUSB from PCSmall LiPo battery — self-contained
Cost~$50 (2004)~$5
Waterproof housingNot sealedPotted or housed in existing polycarbonate lid

Sample rate of 100Hz is more than sufficient — the model roll period of ~2.5 seconds gives 250 samples per cycle at 100Hz. The Nyquist limit for roll harmonics up to 10× fundamental (25Hz) is met with margin. For paired model testing at AMC, a GPS pulse-per-second signal or common trigger wire synchronises the two loggers to a common timebase.

Test Programme — Phase 1: Lake Free Decay

Free decay tests in a local Blue Mountains lake establish the baseline roll damping coefficient ζ for the hull. Flat calm water early morning eliminates wind and wave contamination. Each test takes approximately 5 minutes.

Protocol

  1. Float model at correct draft — verify against calculated waterline
  2. Adjust ballast until KG/D matches target (KG/D ≈ 0.6 — soft GM, long roll period)
  3. Apply known initial heel angle (~15°) — release cleanly
  4. Arduino IMU logs roll angle at 100Hz to SD card
  5. Extract roll period from zero crossings
  6. Extract damping ratio ζ from logarithmic decrement of amplitude envelope
  7. Repeat minimum 3 runs per configuration for repeatability

Test Matrix

ConfigurationPurpose
Bare hull — no keelsBaseline ζ — hull skin friction and bilge damping only
Single centreline keelKeel contribution — isolated
Triple keel — keel + 2 bilge keelsFull configuration — compare against bare hull
Triple keel — varied depthSensitivity of ζ to keel depth
Triple keel — varied widthSensitivity of ζ to keel plan area

Success Criterion

Literature empirical range for keel-fitted vessels: ζ = 0.10–0.18. The triple keel configuration should achieve ζ ≥ 0.10. If bare hull gives ζ ≈ 0.05–0.07 (typical for smooth hull), the keel contribution should approximately double the damping — consistent with empirical data from keel damping literature.

The roll period measured in free decay should match the GM-derived natural period Tn = 2π√(I/GΔ) using the calculated target GM of ~0.85m. Agreement within 10% validates the KG and GM estimates.

Paired Model Concept

Running two models simultaneously in the same wave environment eliminates environmental variability from comparative tests. Both models experience identical wave conditions — any difference in measured response is purely attributable to the design variable under test.

VariableModel AModel B
Hull formShip-shaped (Lovett/Magnuson)Box barge
DampingTriple keelBare hull
WeathervaningStern skeg fittedNo skeg
KGTarget KG (~9.5m full scale)Low KG (stiff vessel)

The AMC Qualisys motion capture system can track both models simultaneously — the paired test is directly supported by the facility. Two synchronised Arduino IMU loggers (GPS or trigger-pulse synchronised) provide independent verification of the optical tracking data.

Scale Modelling Principles

Froude scaling governs model testing for gravity-dominated phenomena (waves, roll, heave). All time-domain quantities scale as √λ where λ is the length scale ratio.

QuantityScale factorExample at 1:79
Length1/λ1/79
Time / period1/√λ1/√79 = 1/8.89
Velocity (wave, wind)1/√λ40kn full → 4.5kn model
Wave height1/λ10m full → 127mm model
Roll period1/√λ22s full → 2.5s model
Mass1/λ³21,000t full → 42kg model
Force1/λ³Scales with mass

Damping ratio ζ is dimensionless — it does not scale. The ζ measured on the model directly equals the full-scale ζ, provided the damping mechanism is correctly reproduced (keel geometry scaled geometrically, same C_d in water). This makes free decay testing particularly valuable — the damping result transfers directly to the full-scale analysis without correction.
