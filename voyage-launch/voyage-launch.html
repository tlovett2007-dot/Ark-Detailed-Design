The launch of Noah's Ark was not a conventional ship launch — the vessel did not slide down a slipway into water. The flood came to the Ark. The engineering challenge was controlling a stationary vessel as floodwaters rose around it, ensuring it floated free without capsizing, snagging, or drifting prematurely.

2. Mooring Rope Through the Moon Pool

The mooring rope passes vertically upward through the moon pool trunk from an anchor point on the ground below the hull, exits at deck level inside the moon pool, and is secured at the top where it can be cut with an axe when required.

This geometry is critical. A conventional surface mooring rope attached to the bow or stern at an angle creates a downward force component as the vessel rises — actively opposing buoyancy at the moment of first float. The steeper the angle, the greater the downward pinning force.

ParameterDiagonal surface ropeVertical moon pool rope
Force direction on hullDiagonal — downward component pins hullPurely vertical — zero downward component until rope goes taut

3. Anti-Capsize Geometry

A rope attached to the centre of the hull bottom and running vertically to a ground anchor provides inherent capsize resistance during the launch phase:

  • If the vessel heels to port, the rope attachment point moves to starboard of the anchor — the rope pulls the hull bottom back toward vertical
  • The restoring moment increases with heel angle — self-correcting geometry
  • No crew action required — purely passive during the critical first-float phase

A surface rope attached at the bow or stern provides no such restoring moment — it may actually contribute to capsize if the vessel heels toward the rope attachment side.

4. Water Level Indicator

The moon pool trunk provides a direct visual indication of external water level. As flood water rises outside the hull, the water level inside the moon pool rises identically — no lag, no estimation. The crew can observe from deck level exactly when:

ObservationSignificance
Water appears in pool bottomFlood has reached hull underside
Water rising in poolFlood rising — buoyancy increasing
Rope goes taut / vessel movement feltFirst float — vessel fully buoyant
Vessel stable and floating clearSafe to cut rope

This eliminates guesswork about when to cut — the crew has real-time water level information from inside the vessel without exposing anyone to the flood exterior.
