The Hebrew word for Ark is tebah.

What does tebah mean?

Can't say for sure. Probably rescue vessel.

In other words: "The etymology of tebah is undetermined. In both occurrences the word functions as a functional terminus technicus describing a vessel whose purpose is the preservation of life."

The Argument in Brief

The box-shaped Ark assumption rests on three independent sources — none of which survive scrutiny:

  1. The Egyptian etymology (Brugsch 1867 → Erman 1892 → BDB 1906) — proposed that tebah derives from Egyptian ḏbꜣ.t (chest/coffin). Fails on four independent grounds: no motive (Hebrew already had aron), semantic mismatch (ḏbꜣ.t never used for boats), phonetic mismatch (one certain, one arguable, one mismatch), and the equation's own author dropped it from his authoritative dictionary in 1926. Cohen (1972) demolished it in print. Nobody rebutted him.
  2. Cassuto's parallelepiped claim (1964) — asserted without philological source that tebah "precisely" means parallelepiped. Modern Hebrew usage cited as confirmation — catastrophically circular (modern Hebrew got the term from the box-reading tradition under examination). Two engineering arguments (cannot rest on flat ground; not navigated therefore not a ship) are demonstrably wrong by engineering standards. Published eight years before Cohen demolished the etymology it rested on.
  3. The LXX translation choice (c.250 BC) — the Alexandrian translators rendered tebah as kibotos (chest/box). The same translators used thibis for Moses' basket — acknowledging that the same word could not consistently mean box across both occurrences. The English word "ark" has no Hebrew ancestry: it is Greek → Latin → English only.

The Targum Onkelos — the authoritative Aramaic translation of the Torah — renders tebah as תֵּיבוּ. This is not a translation. It is the same word in the sister dialect. The Aramaic speakers imposed no geometry because they needed no interpretation. They already had the word.

HALOT (1999) independently reaches Cohen's conclusion via ANE cuneiform scholarship: "decision open"; "vessel was a ship."

The shape of Noah's vessel is not in the word tebah. It is determined by naval architecture. The AiG Ark Encounter — ship-shaped, 157 metres, designed for passive weathervaning in the sea state implied by Genesis — is the engineering consequence of taking Cohen seriously.
