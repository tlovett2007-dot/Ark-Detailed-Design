The Egyptian Etymology of Tebah

A Primary Source Investigation

T. Lovett | 2026 | Part of papers-tbh

The claim that Hebrew tebah (תֵּבָה) derives from an Egyptian word for chest or coffin has been repeated in biblical lexicography for over 150 years. The standard attribution is to Brugsch (1867). This page examines the primary Egyptological sources directly — Brugsch's own Hieroglyphisch-Demotisches Wörterbuch (1867–1882) and Erman and Grapow's Wörterbuch der Ägyptischen Sprache (1926/1931) — to establish what these sources actually say, and what they do not.

The investigation required direct examination of seven volumes of nineteenth and twentieth century Egyptological lexicography. The findings differ substantially from the received tradition.

1. What Brugsch Actually Said

Brugsch's Hieroglyphisch-Demotisches Wörterbuch was published in seven volumes between 1867 and 1882. It is the work invariably cited as the origin of the Egyptian etymology of tebah. The relevant entry is found in Volume 4 (1868) at dictionary page 1628, under the Egyptian root teb.

Brugsch, Heinrich. Hieroglyphisch-Demotisches Wörterbuch. Vol. 4. Leipzig: J.C. Hinrichs, 1868. — Dict. p. 1628, entry: teb / teb-t

Brugsch Vol. 4, dictionary p.1628. Entry for Egyptian teb / teb-t. The Hebrew cross-reference appears in line 3 of the entry.

"die Truhe, der 'Todtenkasten, der Sarkophag,' le cercueil, le sarcophage, la caisse funéraire. Cf. THBE T. T arca sepulthralis."

The chest, the 'coffin, the sarcophagus,' the coffin, the sarcophagus, the funeral casket. Cf. Coptic THBE [Theban dialect] arca sepulthralis.

"Cf. hebr. תָּבָה fem., thald. תַּבּוּתָא, arab. تابوت, griech. θύβη, θήβη, arca, cista."

Cf. Hebrew תָּבָה feminine, Chaldean תַּבּוּתָא, Arabic تابوت, Greek θύβη, θήβη, arca, cista.

Finding 1. Brugsch connects Egyptian teb → Coptic THBE / TAIBL → Hebrew תָּבָה → Arabic تابوت → Greek θύβη. The equation is filed under the Egyptian root TEB, not under ḏb't. The consonant correspondence is T-B → T-B — a direct match. This is phonologically coherent.

1b. Brugsch on Phonological Method

The same volume reveals Brugsch's explicit approach when cross-referencing Egyptian with Semitic cognates. At dictionary page 628 (Vol. 2, 1868), Brugsch discusses the Egyptian word emtebà (finger) and corrects a contemporaneous scholar who had attempted to connect it with tebà:

Brugsch, Heinrich. Hieroglyphisch-Demotisches Wörterbuch. Vol. 2. Leipzig: J.C. Hinrichs, 1868. — Dict. p. 628, entry: emtebà

Brugsch Vol. 2, dictionary p.628. Entry for Egyptian emtebà (finger). Brugsch explicitly rejects Pleyte's phonological equation on the grounds that the words sound differently.

"Hr. Pleyte, Text p. 170, stellt das Wort mit dem ganz anderen lautenden Worte [tebà], dem Prototyp des Kopt. TEB, THB digitus zusammen, wobei er in der Umschreibung seines Textes die Sylbe [sign] ganz übersehen hat. Dies zur Berichtigung."

Mr. Pleyte, Text p.170, places this word together with the quite differently sounding word [tebà], the prototype of the Coptic TEB, THB = digitus (finger), in which he has completely overlooked the syllable [sign] in his transcription. This as a correction.

Finding 2. Brugsch's method for cross-referencing Semitic cognates is explicitly phonological: he rejects Pleyte's equation on the grounds that the words sound differently ("anderen lautenden"). When Brugsch does make the tebah connection at p.1628, he does so from the TEB root — because the phonology works. He does not derive tebah from ḏb't.

2. What Erman and Grapow Said — and Did Not Say

Erman and Grapow's Wörterbuch der Ägyptischen Sprache (published 1926–1931) superseded Brugsch as the authoritative Egyptian dictionary. Cohen (1972) notes that Erman had initially agreed with the Egyptian etymology of tebah in 1892, but later silently omitted it from the Wörterbuch. Direct examination confirms this.

HALOT cites Erman-G. Wb. 5: 561; 261 as sources for the Egyptian etymology. Both pages have been examined.

2a. Erman-Grapow Wb. 5: 261 — entry tb.t

Erman, Adolf, and Hermann Grapow. Wörterbuch der Ägyptischen Sprache. Vol. 5. Leipzig: J.C. Hinrichs, 1931. — p. 261, entry: tb.t

Erman-Grapow Wb. Vol. 5, p.261. The tb.t entry. Attested Saite period only. Cross-references ḏb3.t. No Hebrew tebah.

tb.t — belegt Sa[ite period]. Kasten. vgl. ḏb3.t.

tb.t — attested Saite period. Box/chest. cf. ḏb3.t.

Finding 3. Erman-Grapow's tb.t entry at p.261 gives the meaning as chest/box, attested only from the Saite period (664–332 BC — post-Moses by any chronology). It cross-references ḏb3.t but makes no connection to Hebrew tebah.

2b. Erman-Grapow Wb. 5: 561 — entry ḏb3.t

Erman, Adolf, and Hermann Grapow. Wörterbuch der Ägyptischen Sprache. Vol. 5. Leipzig: J.C. Hinrichs, 1931. — p. 561, entry: ḏb3.t

Erman-Grapow Wb. Vol. 5, pp.560–561. The ḏb3.t entry (shrine/sarcophagus) and adjacent entries. Coptic reflex: TAIBE / TAIBI. No Hebrew tebah.

ḏb3.t — belegt seit Lit.MR. Kopt. TAIBE : TAIBI. Schrein, Sarg.

ḏb3.t — attested since literary Middle Kingdom. Coptic TAIBE : TAIBI. Shrine, sarcophagus.

Finding 4. Erman-Grapow's ḏb3.t entry at p.561 gives the meaning as shrine/sarcophagus, with Coptic reflex TAIBE/TAIBI. It makes no connection to Hebrew tebah. The Coptic form TAIBE does not correspond to Hebrew tebah; the Coptic form that matches Hebrew tebah phonologically is THBE — which Brugsch derives from his teb root, not from ḏb3.t.

3. The HALOT Citation

HALOT (the standard modern Hebrew lexicon) cites the Egyptian etymology as follows:

Koehler, Ludwig, Walter Baumgartner, et al. The Hebrew and Aramaic Lexicon of the Old Testament. Leiden: Brill, 1994–2000. — Entry: תֵּבָה

HALOT entry for תֵּבָה. Section A.2 proposes Egyptian derivation from either ḏb't or tbt, citing Erman-G. Wb. 5: 561; 261.

"The sbst. is a loanword from Egyptian and may be derived either a) from ḏb't shrine, sarcophagus; or b) from tbt chest (see Erman-G. Wb. 5: 561; 261)"

Finding 5. HALOT cites Erman-Grapow Wb. 5:561 and 5:261 as sources for both proposed Egyptian etymologies. Direct examination of both pages shows that neither page makes a connection to Hebrew tebah. HALOT's citation introduces a connection that is not present in the source cited.

4. Summary — What the Sources Actually Say

1867–68 FOUND Brugsch connects Egyptian teb → Coptic THBE/TAIBL → Hebrew תָּבָה → Arabic تابوت. Filed under root TEB. Phonological basis: T-B → T-B. Coherent. Does not derive tebah from ḏb't.
1868 METHOD Brugsch explicitly rejects Pleyte's equation of emtebà with tebà on phonological grounds: "dem ganz anderen lautenden Worte" — the quite differently sounding word. His method is phonological.
1892 ACCEPTED Erman accepts an Egyptian etymology for tebah (in a journal article; details not yet located). Cohen (1972) documents this acceptance.
1906 COPIED BDB embeds the equation: "prob. Egypt. loan-word from t-b-t, chest, coffin." No primary philological argument. Qualifier "probably" signals uncertainty already at this stage.
1926–31 SILENT Erman-Grapow Wörterbuch der Ägyptischen Sprache Vol. 5 published. Neither tb.t (p.261) nor ḏb3.t (p.561) contains any reference to Hebrew tebah. The connection Erman accepted in 1892 is silently abandoned.
1972 DEMOLISHED Cohen formally demolishes the Egyptian etymology on Egyptological and semantic grounds: neither ḏb't nor tbt is ever used for boats in Egyptian texts. The equation "cannot be compared."
1994–2000 COPIED HALOT cites Erman-Grapow Wb. 5:561; 261 as sources for the Egyptian etymology. Direct examination shows neither page makes this connection. The tradition cites a retraction as its authority.
2026 THIS PAGE Primary source examination establishes: Brugsch's equation was teb → tebah, not ḏb't → tebah. Erman-Grapow contains no Hebrew connection at the pages HALOT cites. The phonological correspondence has apparently never been formally evaluated in the literature.

5. The Phonological Problem

The standard claim — that Hebrew tebah derives from Egyptian ḏb't — rests on a proposed consonant correspondence. Brugsch's actual equation rests on a different one. The two can be compared:

Source Egyptian word Consonants Coptic reflex → Hebrew תָּבָה Phonological verdict
Brugsch (1868) p.1628 teb / teb-t T – B THBE / TAIBL T – B – H ✅ T→T, B→B. Direct match. Coptic THBE confirms.
Later tradition (misattributed to Brugsch) ḏb't / ḏb3.t Ḏ – B – (3/') – T TAIBE / TAIBI T – B – H ❌ Ḏ→T (wrong; should give Hebrew Ṭ). 3/'→H (no rule). Coptic TAIBE ≠ Hebrew tebah. T feminine suffix lost.

The phonological correspondence that actually works is Brugsch's own equation: Egyptian teb → Coptic THBE → Hebrew tebah. The tradition that subsequently cited ḏb't as the source departed from Brugsch's actual argument and created a phonologically indefensible equation.

Cohen did not need to address the phonology because the Egyptological grounds were already decisive: ḏb't is never used for boats in Egyptian texts, and cannot therefore be the source of a word used for two life-saving vessels. The phonological failure is an additional and independent objection — one that, to this author's knowledge, has not previously been noted in the literature.

6. Conclusion

The Egyptian etymology of tebah as it appears in modern biblical lexicography rests on a chain of errors:

  1. Brugsch's actual equation was teb → tebah (T-B root, phonologically coherent), not ḏb't → tebah.
  2. The ḏb't equation — the one that generated the "box ark" tradition — was accepted by Erman in 1892 and then silently abandoned by him in the authoritative Wörterbuch of 1926.
  3. HALOT cites the 1926 Wörterbuch as authority for the connection that the Wörterbuch does not make.
  4. The phonological correspondence for ḏb't → tebah fails on two of three consonants and is contradicted by the Coptic reflex of that word (TAIBE, not THBE).
  5. Cohen (1972) demolished the equation on Egyptological and semantic grounds. His conclusion — etymology undetermined — has not been rebutted in the 53 years since publication.

The "box ark" tradition inherits its shape from a word that the primary Egyptological literature does not use for boats, whose phonological correspondence does not work, whose own proponent later retracted it, and whose demolition has stood unchallenged for half a century.
