Cubit: Royal Egyptian Cubit (REC) = 524mm. Supersedes Nippur cubit (518.5mm) of Lovett (2006). AiG Ark Encounter uses 0.518m Nippur cubit, moulded dimensions convention — same vessel, different convention. See hull-cubit for full reconciliation.

Note on draft: Genesis 7:20 states the floodwaters prevailed fifteen cubits upward and the mountains were covered. A coherent interpretation: the fifteen cubits represent the Ark's approximate draft — a vessel 30 cubits high, loaded to approximately half its height, floats at 15 cubits draft. This gives the minimum water depth required for the Ark to pass safely over the highest submerged terrain without grounding. The precision of "fifteen cubits" is explained by the vessel's geometry rather than by an observation Noah could not have made from inside a submerged vessel. See papers-tbh for full discussion.

With Cb = 0.87, the Ark lies at the upper end of full-bodied ship forms and approaches the lower end of barge-like hull forms. A naval architect seeing only "length ~157m, Cb 0.87" would think "very full ship" before thinking "ocean barge." Ocean-going barges have Cb of 0.92 and above.

Independent corroboration: naval architect Jim King (Creation Museum, 2005) proposed Great Lakes bulk carriers as the nearest modern analogue for Noah's Ark. The CAD calculation independently arrived at Cb = 0.87 — exactly Great Lakes bulk carrier territory.
