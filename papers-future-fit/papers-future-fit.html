Proposed title: How Many Animals Fit? A Volume-First Engineering Analysis of Noah's Ark Capacity

Target journal: Answers Research Journal (Georgia Purdom contact)

Argument Summary

  1. The methodology inversion — all prior literature goes animals→volume. This paper goes volume→animals. A genuinely novel approach.
  2. Prior literature — Woodmorappe (1996) is the only serious feasibility study. CMI/Sarfati and AiG are downstream of it. Nobody has gone back to first principles.
  3. Starting point — Genesis dimensions — 300×50×30 cubits at 524mm = 157.2m × 26.2m × 15.72m. External volume ~51,700 m³.
  4. Systematic deductions — structural timber, hull skin, bulkheads, decks, ramps, moon pools, ballast zone, services, fodder, water, family quarters. Each deduction justified from engineering requirements.
  5. The residual — animal zone — ~11,107 m³ after all deductions. This is the constrained answer, not a best-case estimate.
  6. Convergence with AiG baraminology — AiG's ~1,400 kinds derived by independent taxonomy sits within the range derived from volume. Two independent methods, same answer.
  7. Significance — convergence by independent methods is strong evidence. The volume-first methodology provides an engineering upper bound on kind count that AiG baraminology satisfies.

1. The Methodology Inversion

Every prior feasibility study approaches the animal capacity question in the same direction:

Prior approach (animals → volume):
Count animal kinds → estimate cage sizes → sum cage volumes → compare to gross Ark volume → conclude it fits

This paper (volume → animals):
Start from Genesis dimensions → deduct all non-animal volumes systematically → residual = maximum animal zone → back-calculate kind count

The prior approach asks: "Can we fit the animals in?" and searches for a yes. This paper asks: "How many animals can the volume actually support?" and derives the answer from engineering constraints. The result is a constrained upper bound, not a best-case estimate.

This methodology was first conceived by T. Lovett around 2006–2007 during work at the Creation Museum. No published study had approached the question from this direction.

5. The Residual — Animal Zone

After all engineering deductions, the effective animal zone is approximately 11,107 m³ — roughly 17.7% of external volume.

This is a constrained result. Unlike prior literature which assumed most of the gross volume was available for animals, this analysis accounts for every volume consumer with engineering justification. The result cannot be improved by optimistic assumptions — each deduction is a minimum requirement, not a maximum allocation.

6. Convergence with AiG Baraminology

AiG baraminology studies (Lightner, Wood et al., 2012+) estimate approximately 1,400 kinds required on the Ark, derived from taxonomic analysis using hybridisation data, cognitum criteria, and statistical baraminology. This is an entirely independent methodology from volume analysis.

MethodApproachResult
This paper — volume-firstEngineering deduction from Genesis dimensions~1,300–1,700 kinds
AiG baraminologyTaxonomic — hybridisation and statistical analysis~1,400 kinds

AiG's ~1,400 sits within the volume-derived range of ~1,300–1,700. Two independent methods — one engineering, one taxonomic — arrive at the same answer. This convergence is the key finding of the paper.

The convergence is not trivial. The volume-first analysis could have produced a result of 500 kinds (too tight) or 5,000 kinds (too loose) — either of which would have been a significant result. Instead it brackets the independent taxonomic estimate. This is strong evidence that both the Genesis dimensions and the taxonomic analysis are internally consistent.

7. Significance

The volume-first methodology provides something no prior study has offered: an engineering upper bound on kind count. The Ark cannot hold more kinds than the volume-first analysis permits. That the independent taxonomic estimate falls within this bound — rather than exceeding it — is a non-trivial confirmation of feasibility.

Bayesian framing: what is the probability that a vessel whose dimensions were determined independently (Genesis 6:15) would, when subjected to rigorous engineering analysis, yield an animal capacity that brackets the independently-derived taxonomic kind count? The convergence is not inevitable — it requires both sets of numbers to be in the right range simultaneously.

This paper does not prove the flood occurred. It demonstrates that the Genesis account is internally consistent across two independent analytical approaches — engineering and taxonomy — applied to the same vessel.
