tebah — Word Studies

ReferenceDetail
Cohen, Chayim. "Hebrew tbh: Proposed Etymologies." Journal of the Ancient Near Eastern Society 4 (1972): 37–51. Foundational word study. Demolishes the Egyptian loanword (coffin/dbt) etymology propagated in BDB and standard lexicons. Argues tebah is a functional descriptor — a life-preserving vessel — not a geometric one. Author confirmed to T. Lovett (~2004) that he still stood by his conclusions. Cohen deceased 2017 (Ben-Gurion University).
Spoelstra, Joshua Joel. Life Preservation in Genesis and Exodus: An Exegetical Study of the Tēbâ of Noah and Moses. Contributions to Biblical Exegesis and Theology 98. Leuven: Peeters, 2020. 432 pp. ISBN 978-90-429-4071-0. PDF
 Most comprehensive recent treatment of tebah. Examines both occurrences (Gen 6–9 and Exod 2:1–10) synchronically and diachronically. Surveys Babylonian and Egyptian lexemes for source language. Charts recension history through LXX and daughter translations. Concludes tebah is a terminus technicus for a life-preserving receptacle — functional, not geometric. Essential reading for Paper 1.

Septuagint — kibotos and Translation History

ReferenceDetail
Septuagint (LXX) — Genesis 6–9; Exodus 2:1–10 Translates both occurrences of tebah as kibotos (κιβωτός). Also used for the Ark of the Covenant — demonstrating kibotos is functional (protective container) not geometric. Source of the box-Ark assumption in subsequent translation tradition.
Brenton, Sir Lancelot C.L. The Septuagint with Apocrypha: Greek and English. London: Samuel Bagster & Sons, 1851. (Repr. Hendrickson, 1986.) Standard English LXX reference. Used for kibotos usage comparison across Genesis 6, Exodus 2, and Exodus 25 (Ark of the Covenant).

Hebrew Lexicons

ReferenceDetail
Brown, F., Driver, S.R., and Briggs, C.A. A Hebrew and English Lexicon of the Old Testament. Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1906. (BDB) Standard scholarly lexicon. Tebah entry propagates the Egyptian loanword etymology that Cohen (1972) refuted. Important as the source of the error in subsequent scholarship — cited to demonstrate the problem, not as authority on tebah.
Gesenius, Wilhelm. Hebrew and Chaldee Lexicon to the Old Testament Scriptures. Trans. Samuel Prideaux Tregelles. London: Bagster, 1847. Classic lexicon. Pre-Cohen; repeats coffin/chest etymology. Useful for tracing the history of the error.

raqia — Firmament Studies

ReferenceDetail
Septuagint (LXX) — Genesis 1:6–8 Translates rāqîaʿ (Hebrew: spread-out stuff, from rāqaʿ — to spread out) as stereōma (στερέωμα — solid dome). Grammatically parallel but semantically antithetical — a massive error in meaning that imported Greek cosmology into the translation tradition. Source of the flat-earth/solid-dome firmament confusion.

ANE — Babylonian and Egyptian Context

ReferenceDetail
George, Andrew. The Babylonian Gilgamesh Epic: Introduction, Critical Edition and Cuneiform Texts. 2 vols. Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2003. Standard critical edition of the Gilgamesh flood account (Tablet XI). Utnapishtim's vessel: cubic proportions based on the sacred Babylonian iku unit — symbolically constructed, hydrodynamically absurd. Essential contrast with Genesis proportions.
Finkel, Irving. The Ark Before Noah: Decoding the Story of the Flood. London: Hodder & Stoughton, 2014. Proposes a circular coracle Ark based on the Ark Tablet (kippatum argument). The kippatum interpretation is rebutted: the word functions as a verbal construction describing a drafting method, not hull shape. Statham (2006) established the square-vessel reading prior to Finkel's circular interpretation.
