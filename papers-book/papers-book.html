Inconvenient Evidence: The Naval Architecture of Noah's Ark

A proposed second book — the synthesis of the research programme documented on this site, written for a technical but non-specialist audience. Where Noah's Ark: Thinking Outside the Box (Lovett, 2008) opened the argument, this book closes it — with two decades of additional research, the construction of the Ark Encounter as a reference point, and a body of peer-reviewed papers as the scholarly foundation.

The title also echoes the cultural currency of Al Gore's An Inconvenient Truth — the secular academy has its inconvenient truths; the evidence for Noah's Ark has its own. The irony is intentional.

The Narrative Spine — Motivated Skepticism

The unifying thread is a precise and non-polemical observation: motivated skepticism toward inconvenient evidence explains the persistent resistance to taking the Genesis account seriously as an engineering document.

A skeptic who applies skepticism selectively — vigorously to inconvenient evidence, suspended for convenient evidence — is not following the evidence. The tell is consistency: does the same standard of scrutiny apply regardless of where the conclusion points?

Three historical examples establish the pattern before the Ark argument begins:

The book then shows that the Genesis account, read as an engineering specification without motivated skepticism, is consistent with everything naval architecture knows about large timber vessel design. The evidence is inconvenient for the consensus. That is not the evidence's problem.

Target Audience

The book sits between the technical papers and popular creationist literature. It assumes an intelligent reader without naval architecture training — someone who can follow a structural argument when explained clearly but doesn't need the mathematics. Engineers and scientists will find the technical content rigorous; general readers will find it accessible.

Primary audience: creationist and biblically informed readers who want more than devotional material — the technically curious who found Thinking Outside the Box satisfying and want the next level.

Secondary audience: naval architects, maritime historians, and shipbuilding researchers who encounter the book through the papers — the technical credibility of the paper series opens doors that popular creationist publishing does not.
