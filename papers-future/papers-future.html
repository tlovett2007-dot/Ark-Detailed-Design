Hull 9 Paradox — Noted by Hong, Explained by This Study

Hull 9 (shorter, wider: L/1.5 × 1.5B × D) consistently outperforms the biblical Ark in stability and structural safety under Hong's confused sea assumption. This appears to undermine the biblical proportions — until weathervaning is considered.

Paper 1 — Noah Never Built an Ark: Why Tebah Does Not Mean "Box"

ElementDetail
StatusIn development — see working draft
Target journalJournal of Creation / Answers Research Journal
TopicHebrew word study — tebah carries no geometric information; box-Ark assumption enters through Latin arca, not Hebrew

The Hebrew word tebah (תֵּבָה) appears only twice in Scripture — Noah's vessel (Genesis 6–9) and Moses' basket (Exodus 2:3). Cohen (1972) demolished the Egyptian loanword etymology in peer-reviewed philological research; the field has cited his paper for 53 years without engaging his conclusions. Cassuto's "precisely parallelepiped" claim is an unreferenced assertion in a commentary. Lambdin's (1953) specialist Egyptian loanword catalogue omits tebah entirely. Spoelstra (2020), the most recent dedicated treatment, confirms the functional reading but inconsistently accepts the box shape while citing Cohen six times and engaging him zero times. The box-Ark assumption is a transmission error: tebah → kibotos → arca (Jerome) → "Ark" (English). The shape of Noah's vessel comes from naval architecture, not from the word used to describe it.

Paper 5 — Gopher Wood: The Hapax Legomenon

ElementDetail
StatusEarly development — see working draft
Target journalJournal of Creation / Answers Research Journal
TopicGopher (גֹּפֶר) — single occurrence, Genesis 6:14. No cognates, no identification in 2,500 years of scholarship. The silence is evidence.

Gopher wood is a hapax legomenon — one occurrence in the entire Hebrew Bible. Unlike every common ANE timber, which has a well-attested Hebrew name, gopher has resisted identification since the LXX translators encountered it in 250 BC. The LXX retreat to "squared timbers" (a processing description, not a species name) suggests the word had already lost its referent within 2,000 years of the flood. The extinction hypothesis — the flood itself eliminated the species — explains the persistent failure of identification. Naval architecture constraints provide a property profile: large-dimension, flexible, rot-resistant, resinous timber. A full transmission table of proposed identifications is in development.
