Light and Ventilation — Sohar

Genesis 6:16 — One Word, No Consensus

Last revised: July 2026 | Status: In development

Genesis 6:16 contains one of the most contested words in the entire Ark specification:

צֹהַר תַּעֲשֶׂה לַתֵּבָה — sohar ta'aseh la-tebah — "a sohar you shall make for the ark"

The word sohar (צֹהַר) occurs exactly once in the entire Old Testament. No other occurrence exists from which to derive its meaning. Every translation is an interpretation.

The Word Itself

Sohar (צֹהַר) is a hapax legomenon — one occurrence, no cognates firmly established. Two proposed etymological connections exist:

ProposalConnectionProblem
"Noon-brightness" / light featureRoot צהר (tshr) = brightness, specifically noon or midday light. Cognate tsohorayim = "noon/midday" — the time of brightest overhead light (e.g. Gen 43:16, Deut 28:29)The root gives quality of light (noon-brightness), not the structure that admits it. A vanilla reading: "Make a noon-brightness for the ark" — the feature is defined by what it does, not what it is. Consistent with the functional vocabulary of the whole passage.
"Roof"Arabic cognate zuhr — "back, upper surface"Cross-language comparison; Arabic cognate not universally accepted
Precious stone (Midrash)Tradition that Noah used a glowing gem for lightNo textual basis; post-biblical Midrashic invention

Neither the "window" nor the "roof" etymology is established on a sound philological basis. The word is opaque.

The Verse in Context

Genesis 6:16 in full:

צֹהַר תַּעֲשֶׂה לַתֵּבָה וְאֶל־אַמָּה תְּכַלֶּנָּה מִלְמַעְלָה
"A sohar you shall make for the ark, and to a cubit you shall finish it from above."

The LXX Greek text of the full verse (6:16) is:

ἐπισυνάγων ποιήσεις τὴν κιβωτὸν καὶ εἰς πῆχυν συντελέσεις αὐτὴν ἄνωθεν τὴν δὲ θύραν τῆς κιβωτοῦ ποιήσεις ἐκ πλαγίων κατάγαια διώροφα καὶ τριώροφα ποιήσεις αὐτήν

Literal English: "Gathering-together you shall make the chest, and to a cubit you shall complete it from above; and the door of the chest you shall make from the side; lower, two-storied and three-storied you shall make it."

The LXX renders sohar as episynagōn — a present active participle meaning "gathering-together." Not a noun naming a feature, but a participle describing an action or quality. The Alexandrian translators could not identify the word and declined to invent a name for it. They described the function they inferred. This is the honest position: unknown word, functional paraphrase.

Note also the LXX levels: κατάγαια διώροφα καὶ τριώροφα — "lower, two-storied and three-storied." The Hebrew has only lower, second, third — no noun. The LXX supplies "storied" from construction context, exactly as it supplies "gathering" for sohar. Both are inferences from context, not translations of specific words.

The phrase "to a cubit you shall finish it from above" is itself contested:

InterpretationReading
Continuous opening running around the hull, one cubit deep, just below the roof lineA ventilation/light strip — clerestory
A single window, one cubit squareA small opening
The cubit refers to the roof overhang above the openingA covered opening protected from rain
The sohar is the roof itself, finished to a cubit thicknessA structural specification

Engineering Analysis

Whatever sohar means, the engineering requirements of the Ark provide constraints:

RequirementImplication for sohar
Light for 371 days — no electricitySome form of light admission required on all three decks
Ventilation for thousands of animalsSubstantial air exchange required — a single small window is insufficient
Watertight in storm seasAny opening must be closeable or protected from wave action
All systems passive — 8 people cannot manage complex ventilationNatural convection preferred over mechanical systems
Weathervaning — waves from astern in Phase 3Openings on lee side (bow) protected; stern openings exposed to following seas

A continuous clerestory opening running around the hull just below the roof line — one cubit (524mm) deep — satisfies all these requirements simultaneously:

  • Provides light to the upper deck directly and via reflection to lower decks
  • Provides substantial ventilation area — 157m perimeter × 0.524m = ~82m² of opening
  • Protected from rain and spray by the roof overhang
  • Can be partially closed by shutters in storm conditions
  • Passive — no crew management required in normal conditions

This interpretation is consistent with "to a cubit you shall finish it from above" — the roof overhangs the opening by one cubit, providing weather protection.

Sohar as Antediluvian Vocabulary

Sohar belongs to the cluster of Genesis 1–9 words whose etymology is permanently unresolvable because the referent is pre-flood. The word describes a specific architectural feature of a pre-flood vessel — a feature that may not have had a post-flood equivalent in ordinary boat construction.

The LXX translators (c.250 BC) rendered it episynagōn — gathering-together, as a present active participle — declining to name the feature and instead describing what it does. Their choice implies something more complex than a single opening: a feature that gathers or collects, most plausibly light and air, by its design.

Like gopher, kopher II, and tebah, the search for a satisfactory post-flood etymology has failed. The word occurs once, in a pre-flood context, describing a feature of the most unusual vessel ever built. The honest answer is: unknown. See antediluvian vocabulary for the framework.
